VERDICT vs "each CU can receive at most 32 bytes per cycle":
- Hot dwordx4 (W16) at 4 waves sustains 63.8/63.9 B/core-cycle gross (~2x the claim);
  hot dword at 8 waves sustains 62.9 B/core-cycle; LDS b128 delivers 125.3 B/core-cycle.
- Hot dwordx2 (W8) saturates near 31.9 B/core-cycle: dwordx2 and dwordx4 take the SAME
  ~64 cycles per instruction at 4 waves, so the limiter is per-instruction, not per-byte.
- Dup sweep: W16 cycles/load change by only 0.3 cycles between 1024 B and 16 unique B
  per instruction -> the hot ceiling is NOT unique-byte delivery.
- The fp8-w8a16 fixed-probe "32 B/core-cycle" is therefore not a universal CU receive ceiling;
  it is consistent with a per-instruction global-load return limit (~1 wide load per ~14-16 CU
  cycles, ~1 dword load per ~4-5 cycles) acting on its 8-loads-per-step pattern, plus its
  conversion/copy VALUs. No hardware counters were used; the limiting unit is not identified.
